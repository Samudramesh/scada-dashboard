    Engineering Principles

    How Samudramesh Upgrades BMC Trash Booms

    1. Diagonal Deflection Geometry: Instead of placing booms perpendicular (which dams the flow), the diagonal 45° angle uses natural stream velocity to slide plastic toward the collector pockets.
    2. Bi-Directional Non-Return Trapping: South Juhu studies demonstrated that the flood tide pushes plastic landward. Pockets A and B capture debris whether water flows seaward or backward.
    3. Continuous High-Volume Lift: Cleated dewatering conveyor lifts saturated plastic out of the water immediately, avoiding waterlogged mass buildup on the curtain boom.

    01 · Watershed Problem

    The Drain as a Transport Vector

    1. Kerbside Inflow: Single-use food packaging and bags wash into road drains during first-flush monsoon showers.
    2. Tidal Sloshing: Semi-diurnal tides reverse hydraulic gradients, repeatedly churning debris back and forth against culvert walls.
    3. Mechanical Shredding: Solar UV embrittlement and hydraulic turbulence shatter plastic into secondary microplastics within days.
    4. Open Marine Escape: Once debris crosses creek mouths into surf zones, cleanup costs escalate over 40-fold.
